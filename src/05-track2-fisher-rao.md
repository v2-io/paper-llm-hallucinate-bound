## Track 2: the universal Fisher-Rao constant under (PI) + (R) + (K) ^sec-track2

The no-go of [[#^sec-no-go]] tells us what to commit to. The minimal commitment that delivers a universal *dimension-free* constant turns out to be a *triple* — sufficient-statistic invariance + Riemannian structure + KL as the bound's information coordinate — under which Čencov's 1982 uniqueness theorem forces Fisher-Rao with constant $\sqrt{2}$.

### The (PI), (R), (K) axioms ^sec-track2-axioms

**(PI) Sufficient-statistic invariance.** *The bias bound's metric on $\mathcal{M}$ is invariant under sufficient statistics — equivalently, under Markov morphisms / congruent embeddings of statistical manifolds in the sense of Čencov 1982.*

(PI) is a working axiom, not a derived result. The strong form here — invariance under sufficient statistics, not merely smooth coordinate change — is what Čencov's uniqueness theorem actually requires. Many Riemannian metrics are invariant under smooth coordinate change; the Fisher information metric is the unique one (up to scalar) invariant under the strictly stronger Markov-morphism class. Smooth-coordinate invariance alone would not single out Fisher-Rao. We name (PI) in its strong form to make the Čencov-uniqueness move below airtight.

**(R) Riemannian structure.** *The bound's metric $d_{\mathcal{M}}$ derives from a smooth Riemannian metric on $\mathcal{M}$.* This filters out divergence forms (TV, Hellinger-as-divergence, $\chi^2$-as-divergence) — those *also* satisfy (PI) as $f$-divergences but are not metric distances on $\mathcal{M}$ in the Riemannian sense. (R) is implicit in the [[#^sec-track1]] / [[#^sec-track2]] framing's "$\mathbb{E}\,d \leq C\sqrt{I}$" theorem-shape with second-order matching; we name it to keep the uniqueness-theorem hypotheses transparent.

**(K) KL second-order matching (normalization condition).** *The bound's metric $d_{\mathcal{M}}$ is normalized to agree with KL at second order at coincident measures: $\mathrm{KL}(P_\theta \,\Vert\, P_{\theta+\delta}) = \tfrac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}^2(P_\theta, P_{\theta+\delta}) + O(d_{\mathcal{M}}^3)$.* This fixes the global scalar that Čencov leaves free (see [[#^thm-fr-uniqueness]] below) and pins the bound's constant $C$ at $\sqrt{2}$. (K) is *not* an additional axiom beyond Track 2's structure: Step 1 of the [[#^sec-track1]] / [[#^sec-track2]] cascade generates conditional KL on the RHS, and Step 2 of Track 2 applies the slice-wise expansion $d_{FR}^2 \le 2\mathrm{KL}(1+o(1))$; the second-order coefficient $\tfrac{1}{2}$ in this expansion (\citealt{amari-nagaoka-2000} §3.7 Theorem 3.1) is exactly what (K) crystallizes as a normalization. Equivalently, (K) filters out switching to a different $f$-divergence on the right-hand side ($\chi^2$, Rényi-$\alpha$, Hellinger-as-divergence) — switching breaks the cascade upstream of the metric choice, since Step 1's chain-rule identity [[#^eq-chain-rule]] is specific to KL and the conditional MI it generates. Like (R), (K) is built into the cascade's upstream structure rather than added as an extra hypothesis; we name it explicitly so the uniqueness statement of [[#^sec-track2-uniqueness]] has its hypotheses fully named.

(PI)'s motivation is that a goal-conditional bias bound is a statement about how far the model's *belief state* moves under goal-coupling — and the belief state is, structurally, a property of the underlying probability distributions, not of any particular description of them. (R) and (K) are not additional commitments beyond what the [[#^sec-track1]] / [[#^sec-track2]] framing already implicitly assumes; they are made explicit here so that the uniqueness statement of [[#^sec-track2-uniqueness]] has its hypotheses fully named.

### Čencov uniqueness and the universal $\sqrt{2}$ ^sec-track2-uniqueness

Under (PI) + (R) and (H1) — the statistical-manifold sub-case — Čencov's theorem \cite{cencov-1982} (modern treatment in \citealt[Theorem 5.1]{ay-jost-le-schwachhofer-2017}) forces the Fisher information metric as the unique (up to a global positive scalar) Riemannian metric on $\mathcal{M}$ invariant under sufficient statistics. Constraint (K) then fixes the scalar to one — the second-order coefficient of the metric must agree with the second-order expansion of KL, which forces $c = 1$. Lebanon's extended Čencov-Campbell characterization \cite{lebanon-2004} generalizes the move to the conditional-information-geometry setting where the goal-conditional bias quantity [[#^eq-bias-quantity]] lives.

> [!theorem] Uniqueness and sharpness of the Fisher-Rao + $\sqrt{2}$ bound ^thm-fr-uniqueness
> Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a statistical-manifold sub-case (H1), and let the bound take the umbrella form $\mathbb{E}\,d_{\mathcal{M}}(\Delta M_{\text{bias}}) \le C\sqrt{I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})}$ under the constraints (PI) + (R) + (K). Then:
> *(a) **Uniqueness of the metric.*** $d_{\mathcal{M}}$ is uniquely the Fisher-Rao geodesic distance.
> *(b) **Sharpness of the constant.*** No constant $C < \sqrt{2}$ uniformly bounds $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}/\sqrt{I_M}$ across the (H4$'$) regime: there exist conjugate-Gaussian Class 1 (Separated) instances on which $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}^2 / I_M \to 2$ as $\mathrm{Var}_G(\beta) \to 0$ with $I_M$ held fixed ([[#^sec-track1-conjugate-gauss]] / [[#^sec-conjugate-gauss-numerics]]). Hence $C = \sqrt{2}$ is the unique sharp upper-bound constant under (PI) + (R) + (K) + (H4$'$).

> [!proof]
> *Proof of (a).* (PI) + (R) invokes Čencov's uniqueness theorem \cite{cencov-1982}\cite[Theorem 5.1]{ay-jost-le-schwachhofer-2017}: on $\mathcal{M}$, the Fisher information metric $\mathbf{I}$ is the unique (up to a global positive scalar $c > 0$) Riemannian metric invariant under Markov morphisms. Write $d_{\mathcal{M}} = \sqrt{c}\,d_{FR}$ where $d_{FR}$ is at the Amari-Nagaoka normalization $\mathbf{I}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}[\nabla^2 \log p_\theta]$. The KL-to-Fisher second-order expansion (\citealt{amari-nagaoka-2000} §3.7 Theorem 3.1) gives $\mathrm{KL}(P_\theta \Vert P_{\theta+\delta}) = \tfrac{1}{2} d_{FR}^2 + O(d_{FR}^3)$, so $\tfrac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}^2 = (c/2)d_{FR}^2 + O(d_{FR}^3)$. Constraint (K) requires $\tfrac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{M}}^2$ agree with $\mathrm{KL}$ at second order, forcing $c = 1$ and $d_{\mathcal{M}} = d_{FR}$.
>
> *Proof of (b).* The conjugate-Gaussian Class 1 (Separated) family of [[#^sec-track1-conjugate-gauss]] with $\mathrm{Var}_G(\beta) \to 0$ and $I_M = \mathrm{Var}_G(\beta)/(2\sigma^2)$ held fixed has $\mathbb{E}[d_{FR}^2(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid G}, P_{M_{\tau^+}})] = \mathrm{Var}_G(\beta)/\sigma^2 = 2 I_M$ exactly (verified in [[#^sec-conjugate-gauss-numerics]]). Hence $\sup\{ \mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}^2/I_M \;:\; \text{instances satisfying (H1) + (PI) + (R) + (K) + (H4$'$)} \} \ge 2$, so any candidate constant $C^2 < 2$ fails on this family. Combined with (a), $C = \sqrt 2$ is the unique sharp upper-bound constant. $\square$

The upshot: once we adopt (PI) at full Markov-morphism strength, plus Riemannian structure, plus KL as the cascade's information coordinate, the *only* such-invariant choice of norm on $\mathcal{M}$ is Fisher-Rao, and the *unique sharp* upper-bound constant is $\sqrt{2}$ — sharper readings of "no further freedom" than the bare Čencov-up-to-scale statement, made literal by the conjugate-Gaussian saturation. Alternative chart-independent commitments — TV with Pinsker, Hellinger-as-divergence, $\chi^2$, Rényi — give weaker bounds: TV-Pinsker is non-tight (the constant is universal but the inequality is loose except at coincident measures); Hellinger-as-divergence and $\chi^2$-as-divergence violate (R); switching divergences on the RHS violates (K). The global [[#^thm-track2-glob]]'s $\pi/\sqrt{2}$ is also sharp in its own (PI)-only regime: it is achieved in the limit $\mathrm{Hel} \to 1$ at the unit $L^2$-sphere's antipode, i.e., when goals can drive the post-update measure to a near-disjoint support from the marginal — outside (H4$'$) but inside (PI).

*Scope on the statistical-manifold class.* Čencov's uniqueness theorem applies to dominated finite-dimensional regular families and (in the discrete case) categorical alphabets; the modern formulation of \citet[Theorem 5.1]{ay-jost-le-schwachhofer-2017} extends to the natural sigma-finite settings used here. (H1) explicitly invokes the statistical-manifold sub-case to keep this scope visible; LLM token-distribution belief states fall in the discrete-categorical case (with the simplex as the manifold), which is well-inside the Čencov regime.

### KL-to-Fisher second-order expansion ^sec-track2-kl-fr

For nearby distributions $P, Q$ on a statistical manifold with Fisher metric $\mathbf{I}$, the KL divergence admits the second-order expansion (\citealt{cover-thomas-2006} §12.5; \citealt{amari-nagaoka-2000} §3.7 Theorem 3.1):

$$\mathrm{KL}(P \,\Vert\, Q) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot d_{FR}^2(P, Q) + O\bigl(d_{FR}^3(P, Q)\bigr).$$ ^eq-kl-fr-expansion

This is the infinitesimal form of the Bregman divergence on the exponential-family Fenchel geometry; it is exact at second order and tight in the small-information regime.

We invoke [[#^eq-kl-fr-expansion]] under one additional hypothesis:

**(H4$'$) Uniform local regime.** $\mathrm{ess\,sup}_g\, \mathrm{KL}(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}, G=g} \,\Vert\, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}}) \le \delta_\star$ for some $\delta_\star \in (0, 1)$, so that *every* goal-conditional slice — not merely the average — is uniformly close to the goal-marginal on the statistical manifold and the second-order expansion [[#^eq-kl-fr-expansion]] is sharp slice-wise. The remainder is controlled uniformly: under bounded Amari-Chentsov tensor $\mid T_{ijk}\delta^i\delta^j\delta^k\mid \le \mathcal{T}_\star\Vert\delta\Vert^3$ on the goal-induced neighborhood and minimum Fisher eigenvalue $\mathbf{I}_{\min} > 0$, the slice-wise inequality $d_{FR}^2(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid G=g}, P_{M_{\tau^+}}) \le 2\,\mathrm{KL}_g(1 + R_3(\delta_\star))$ holds with $R_3(\delta_\star) = (\mathcal{T}_\star/3)\sqrt{2\delta_\star/\mathbf{I}_{\min}^3} \to 0$ as $\delta_\star \to 0$.

This is *strictly stronger* than the small-mean condition $\mathbb{E}_G[\mathrm{KL}_g] = I \ll 1$ — small mean does not imply small ess-sup, and rare goals carrying disproportionately large slice-wise KL would otherwise leave the third-order remainder uncontrolled. (H4$'$) implies $I \le \delta_\star$, so the small-mean condition is automatic. Operationally (H4$'$) corresponds to *moderate goal-coupling on every goal*, not just on average — the cleaner reading of the small-information regime. When (H4$'$) fails (rare goals carry large transferred information), the slice-wise expansion is no longer uniformly sharp; for that regime the global [[#^thm-track2-glob]] below recovers a non-asymptotic universal-constant bound at the cost of $\sqrt 2 \mapsto \pi/\sqrt 2$ on the constant. Macroscopic hallucination — outputs that bear no relation to the evidence — also lives outside (H4$'$) and is covered by [[#^thm-track2-glob]].

### The Track 2 bound — one step ^sec-track2-bound

**Step 1 — KL-to-Fisher-Rao slice-wise + chain rule on the post-update.** Apply [[#^eq-kl-fr-expansion]] under (H1) + (H4$'$) slice-wise at each $G = g$ to get $d_{FR}^2(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e,M,G=g}, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e,M}) \leq 2\mathrm{KL}(\cdots)(1+o(1))$. Take expectation over $G$ and substitute the chain-rule identity [[#^eq-chain-rule]]:

$$\mathbb{E}\bigl[d_{FR}^2(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}, G},\, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}})\bigr] \;\leq\; 2 \cdot I(G;\,M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-}) \cdot (1 + o(1)).$$

Taking square roots and applying Jensen:

> [!theorem] Track 2, unconditional ^thm-track2-uncond
> Under (H1), (H4$'$), and (PI) + (R) + (K), the locally-tight Fisher-Rao bound [[#^eq-track2-uncond]] holds. The constant $C_{FR} = \sqrt{2}$ is universal, dimension-free, and carries no domain-specific parameters.

$$\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}\bigl(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}, G},\, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}}\bigr) \;\leq\; \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{I(G;\,M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-})} \cdot (1 + o(1)).$$ ^eq-track2-uncond

> [!corollary] Track 2, architectural factorization ^cor-track2-arch
> Under (H1), (H4$'$), (PI) + (R) + (K), and (H$_\kappa$), the architectural factorization [[#^eq-track2-arch]] holds.

$$\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}\bigl(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}, G},\, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}}\bigr) \;\leq\; \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{\kappa_{\text{processing}} \cdot I(G;\,\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-})} \cdot (1 + o(1)).$$ ^eq-track2-arch

The Track 2 cascade is now a single step. The chain rule on the post-update law (Step 1 of [[#^sec-track1]]) combined with the KL-to-Fisher slice-wise expansion delivers the bound directly; there is no separate posterior-pushforward step, and (H$_\kappa$) re-enters only as the corollary-level architectural commitment that recovers the κ × 𝒜 factorization.

**Beyond (H4$'$): the global Fisher-Rao backstop.** [[#^thm-track2-uncond]] is *locally* tight: it delivers the universal $\sqrt 2$ in the small-information regime (H4$'$). Outside that regime — when a few rare goals carry disproportionately large transferred information, or when the operating distribution genuinely sits at moderate-to-large $I$ — the slice-wise expansion's $(1+o(1))$ remainder is no longer uniformly sharp. A *global* Fisher-Rao bound is available under strictly weaker hypotheses, at the cost of a slightly larger constant:

> [!theorem] Track 2 global backstop, no (H4$'$) and no (R) or (K) ^thm-track2-glob
> Under (H1) and (PI) — for all values of transferred information — the globally-valid Fisher-Rao bound [[#^eq-track2-glob]] holds. The constant $\pi/\sqrt{2} \approx 2.22$ is universal, dimension-free, and global — no small-information condition required, and (R), (K) are not invoked.

$$\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}\bigl(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}, G},\, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}}\bigr) \;\leq\; \frac{\pi}{\sqrt 2} \cdot \sqrt{I(G;\,M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-})}.$$ ^eq-track2-glob

> [!proof]
> On the unit $L^2$-sphere of $\sqrt{p}$, the chord-arc identity ([[#^sec-hellinger-fr-relationship]]) gives $\mathrm{Hel}(P, Q) = \sqrt{2}\sin(d_{FR}(P, Q)/4)$ exactly. [[#^lem-chord-arc]] (in [[#^sec-hellinger-fr-relationship]]) shows $d_{FR}(P, Q) \le \pi\,\mathrm{Hel}(P, Q)$ globally, with equality at the antipode $\mathrm{Hel} = 1$ — the function $h \mapsto 4\arcsin(h/\sqrt{2})/h$ is increasing on $(0, 1]$ from $2\sqrt{2}$ to $\pi$, by strict convexity of $g(h) := 4\arcsin(h/\sqrt 2)$ on $(0, \sqrt 2)$. By \citet[Lemma 2.4]{tsybakov-2009}, $2\,\mathrm{Hel}^2 \le \mathrm{KL}$ slice-wise. Combining: $d_{FR}^2 \le \pi^2\mathrm{Hel}^2 \le (\pi^2/2)\,\mathrm{KL}$ slice-wise. Take $\mathbb{E}_G$, substitute the chain rule [[#^eq-chain-rule]]: $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}^2 \le (\pi^2/2)\,I$. Jensen on $\sqrt{\,\cdot\,}$: $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR} \le (\pi/\sqrt{2})\sqrt I$. $\square$

> [!corollary] Track 2 global backstop, architectural form ^cor-track2-glob-arch
> Under (H1), (PI), and (H$_\kappa$), $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR} \le (\pi/\sqrt 2)\sqrt{\kappa_{\text{processing}}\cdot I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})}.$

[[#^thm-track2-uncond]] (locally tight, $\sqrt 2$, requires (H4$'$) + (R) + (K)) and [[#^thm-track2-glob]] (globally valid, $\pi/\sqrt 2$, requires only (PI)) are companions. The two cover Fisher-Rao at *all* scales — the former wins at small $I$ where (H4$'$) holds; the latter is the natural fallback at moderate-to-large $I$. The factor $\pi/2 \approx 1.57$ is the worst-case cost of giving up the small-information regime — exactly the maximum arc-chord ratio at the unit $L^2$-sphere's antipode. The Hellinger backstop ([[#^thm-hellinger]] below) is the *chord* form; [[#^thm-track2-glob]] is the *arc* form on the same geometry.

### The Hellinger companion to [[#^thm-track2-glob]] ^sec-track2-hellinger

The global Fisher-Rao bound ([[#^thm-track2-glob]]) was proved by combining the chord-arc identity $d_{FR} \le \pi\,\mathrm{Hel}$ with Tsybakov's $2\,\mathrm{Hel}^2 \le \mathrm{KL}$. Stripping the Fisher-Rao step gives a *direct Hellinger statement* with a cleaner universal constant — this is [[#^thm-hellinger]] — which is the chord companion to [[#^thm-track2-glob]]'s arc bound:

> [!theorem] Hellinger backstop ^thm-hellinger
> Under (H1) and (PI) — for all values of transferred information — the Hellinger bound [[#^eq-hellinger-glob]] holds. Under (H$_\kappa$), the architectural factorization $\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{Hel} \leq (1/\sqrt{2})\sqrt{\kappa_{\text{processing}} \cdot I(G;\,\Omega \mid e, M)}$ recovers.

$$\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{Hel}\bigl(P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}, G},\, P_{M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-}}\bigr) \;\leq\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \sqrt{I(G;\,M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau,\, M_{\tau^-})}.$$ ^eq-hellinger-glob

> [!proof]
> By \citet[Lemma 2.4]{tsybakov-2009}, $2\mathrm{Hel}^2(P, Q) \leq \mathrm{KL}(P \Vert Q)$ globally for all probability measures. Apply slice-wise at each $G = g$, take expectation over $G$, and substitute the chain rule [[#^eq-chain-rule]]. $\square$

Hellinger distance satisfies (PI) — it is an $f$-divergence with $f(t) = (\sqrt{t}-1)^2/2$ preserved under Markov morphisms (Csiszár 1967; Liese-Vajda 1987) — and is locally proportional to Fisher-Rao on the unit-sphere representation. We adopt throughout the *standard statistician's* Hellinger convention $\mathrm{Hel}^2(P,Q) := \tfrac{1}{2}\Vert\sqrt p - \sqrt q\Vert_{L^2}^2 = 1 - \int\sqrt{pq}$, so $\mathrm{Hel} \in [0, 1]$ with the global inequality $2\mathrm{Hel}^2 \le \mathrm{KL}$ used in the proof above (\citealt[Lemma 2.4]{tsybakov-2009}); and the *Amari-Nagaoka* Fisher-Rao convention $d_{FR}(P,Q) = 2\arccos\int\sqrt{pq}$ — twice the great-circle arc on the unit sphere of $\sqrt p$ in $L^2$ — so $d_{FR} \in [0, \pi]$. The two are related by $\mathrm{Hel} = \sqrt{2}\,\sin(d_{FR}/4)$, giving the small-distance asymptotic $\mathrm{Hel} \approx d_{FR}/(2\sqrt{2})$ as $d_{FR} \to 0$, and the global chord-arc bound $d_{FR} \le \pi\,\mathrm{Hel}$ via [[#^lem-chord-arc]].

The three Fisher-Rao-spine bounds — [[#^thm-track2-uncond]] (locally tight, $\sqrt 2$, requires (H4$'$) + (R) + (K)), [[#^thm-track2-glob]] (globally valid, $\pi/\sqrt 2$, requires only (PI)), and [[#^thm-hellinger]] (Hellinger chord, $1/\sqrt 2$, requires only (PI)) — share the same intrinsic geometry and have strictly nested hypotheses. There is no "metric switch" between regimes: macroscopic hallucination is in Fisher-Rao directly via [[#^thm-track2-glob]], with [[#^thm-hellinger]] the chord companion on the same unit $L^2$-sphere. [[#^thm-hellinger]] is *not* a fallback for "the regime [[#^thm-track2-uncond]] cannot reach" — that regime is now covered by [[#^thm-track2-glob]] in the natural metric — but a useful direct statement on the chord, with the cleanest universal constant $1/\sqrt 2$, that is implied by the chord-arc-decomposition proof of [[#^thm-track2-glob]] and stands on its own under the same hypotheses.

### Euclidean translations for parametric families ^sec-track2-euclidean

When (H1) is realized by a parametric family with known Fisher information, the Track 2 Fisher-Rao bound translates to an explicit Euclidean bound on parameter displacement. The conjugate-Gaussian case is paradigmatic and review-defusing: *Euclidean displacement does not blow up* in the prior-dominant limit, contrary to a naive reading of the no-go.

> [!theorem] Conjugate-Gaussian Euclidean bound ^thm-conjugate-gauss-euclidean
> Under the conjugate-Gaussian setup of [[#^sec-track1-conjugate-gauss]] ($\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2)$, $\Omega \mid \theta \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta, \sigma^2)$, goal-conditional likelihood-mean shift $\Omega \mid G \sim \mathcal{N}(\beta(G), \sigma^2)$), the post-update parameter law is the Gaussian $\mu_+ \mid G \sim \mathcal{N}(L_{\text{post}}\beta(G),\, L_{\text{post}}^2\sigma^2)$ with $L_{\text{post}} = \tau^2/(\sigma^2+\tau^2)$. The Fisher-Rao bound ([[#^thm-track2-uncond]]) on the manifold of these parameter laws translates to the Euclidean bound on random-parameter displacement [[#^eq-conjugate-gauss-euclidean]]; the prefactor $L_{\text{post}}\,\sigma = \tau^2\sigma/(\sigma^2+\tau^2) \le \tau/2$ uniformly. Under (H$_\kappa$), the architectural form is $\mathbb{E}\,\Vert\Delta\mu_+\Vert_{\text{Eucl}} \leq L_{\text{post}}\sigma\sqrt{2\kappa_{\text{processing}} I(G;\Omega \mid e, M)}(1+o(1))$.

$$\mathbb{E}\,\Vert\Delta \mu_+\Vert_{\text{Eucl}} \;\leq\; L_{\text{post}}\,\sigma \cdot \sqrt{2 \cdot I(G;\,M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})} \cdot (1 + o(1)).$$ ^eq-conjugate-gauss-euclidean

> [!proof]
> The post-update parameter law $\mu_+\mid G$ is Gaussian with mean $L_{\text{post}}\beta(G)$ and variance $L_{\text{post}}^2\sigma^2$ (variance preserved across goals because $\sigma_{\text{post}}^2$ is fixed by the conjugate-update structure and the data-noise variance $\sigma^2$ is goal-invariant). The Fisher metric on the manifold $\{\mathcal{N}(\mu, L_{\text{post}}^2\sigma^2) : \mu \in \mathbb{R}\}$ in the $\mu$-coordinate is $\mathbf{I}(\mu) = 1/(L_{\text{post}}^2\sigma^2)$, so the Fisher-Rao geodesic distance between two such laws is $d_{FR}(\mu_1, \mu_2) = \mid\mu_1-\mu_2\mid/(L_{\text{post}}\sigma)$, a flat metric pulled back from $\mathbb{R}$. Hence $\Vert\Delta\mu_+\Vert_{\text{Eucl}} = L_{\text{post}}\sigma \cdot d_{FR}$. Take expectations and substitute [[#^thm-track2-uncond]]. $\square$

The prefactor $L_{\text{post}}\sigma = \tau^2\sigma/(\sigma^2+\tau^2)$ is bounded above by $\tau/2$ uniformly (AM-GM at the maximizer $\sigma = \tau$) and *vanishes in both extreme limits*: $L_{\text{post}}\sigma \to 0$ as $\sigma \to \infty$ (very noisy observations: the architecture learns essentially nothing from $\Omega$ regardless of goal, so the posterior-mean displacement vanishes) and $L_{\text{post}}\sigma \to 0$ as $\sigma \to 0$ (very sharp observations: $L_{\text{post}}\to 1$ but $\sigma\to 0$, so even though the posterior tracks data tightly the data variance vanishes). Maximum displacement-bound occurs at balanced prior-likelihood tension $\sigma=\tau$. This is *strictly stronger* than a "$\sigma_{\text{post}}\sqrt{2I}$" reading would suggest (which would diverge along the wrong axis): the no-go's $\sigma$-divergence pathology is genuinely defused — Euclidean displacement on $\mu_+$ stays bounded across all $(\sigma, \tau)$. The [[#^sec-no-go]] no-go still applies as stated to *charts*: $L_{\text{post}}\sigma$ is family-specific, and reparameterizing the conjugate-Gaussian setup to natural-parameter coordinates would not yield a coordinate-independent universal constant.

A general exponential-family Euclidean translation ([[#^thm-exp-family]]: $\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert_{\text{Eucl}} \leq \mathbf{I}_{\min}^{-1/2}\sqrt{2\kappa I}$, with proof in [[#^sec-parametric-euclidean-translations]]) recovers dimension-free finite Euclidean bounds for Bernoulli on the logit chart, multinomial on the log-ratio chart, and other natural-parameter charts on bounded-Fisher-curvature manifolds; scale-family parametrizations (Gaussian-$\sigma$, exponential-$\lambda$) recover the no-go's $\mathbf{I}_{\min}^{-1/2} \to \infty$ pathology, consistent with [[#^sec-no-go]].

### The two tracks unified at the distance level ^sec-track2-unified

Track 1 gives $\mathbb{E}\,W_2^2 \leq C_{T_2} \cdot I_M$ (squared distance, linear in transferred information $I_M := I(G; M_{\tau^+}\mid e, M_{\tau^-})$); Track 2 gives $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR}^2 \leq 2 \cdot I_M \cdot (1+o(1))$ (squared Fisher-Rao distance, linear in $I_M$). Both tracks deliver the *same shape* — squared distance, linear in $I_M$ — and Jensen lifts each to the *distance, square-root* shape of the umbrella bound [[#^eq-umbrella]]: $\mathbb{E}\,W_2 \leq \sqrt{C_{T_2} I_M}$ for Track 1 and $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR} \leq \sqrt{2 I_M}$ for Track 2. The two are not "different scalings"; they are bounds on *different metrics* with the *same* information dependence.

The differences are in the metric and the constant. Track 1 bounds $W_2$, requires (H1) + (H2$'$) — a Talagrand $T_2$ inequality on the post-update law — and carries a constant $C_{T_2}$ that depends on the post-update concentration ($C_{T_2} = 2/\rho_{\text{post}}$ under route (a)). Track 2 bounds $d_{FR}$ at *two* universal constants: $\sqrt 2$ locally tight under (H1) + (H4$'$) + (PI) + (R) + (K) ([[#^thm-track2-uncond]]), and $\pi/\sqrt 2$ globally valid under (H1) + (PI) only ([[#^thm-track2-glob]]). Track 1 is operative when the post-update law is concentrated but the architecture lives off the statistical-manifold sub-case or violates (PI); Track 2's local form is operative when the statistical-manifold sub-case holds, the (PI) + (R) + (K) triple is committed to, and the operating regime is uniform-locality; Track 2's global form is operative whenever (H1) + (PI) hold, regardless of $I_M$. [[#^thm-hellinger]] is the chord companion to [[#^thm-track2-glob]] on the same unit $L^2$-sphere — universal $1/\sqrt 2$ on Hellinger, available under (H1) + (PI) alone, with the $\sqrt 2$ improvement in the local regime via [[#^lem-chord-arc]] + [[#^thm-track2-uncond]] (giving $(1/2)\sqrt{I_M}$ on Hellinger at small $I_M$). At intermediate scales where multiple tracks apply, the operative bound is the tighter of those active under the realized metric.

For the conjugate-Gaussian worked example of [[#^sec-track1-conjugate-gauss]], with $\tau = \sigma = 1$ (balanced, $L_{\text{post}} = 1/2$, $L_{\text{post}}^2\sigma^2 = 1/4$, $L_{\text{post}}\sigma = 1/2$): Track 1 gives $\mathbb{E}\,W_2 \leq \sqrt{I_M/2}$ (with $C_{T_2} = 1/2$); Track 2 gives $\mathbb{E}\,d_{FR} \leq \sqrt{2 I_M}$; [[#^thm-conjugate-gauss-euclidean]] Euclidean is $\mathbb{E}\,\Vert\Delta\mu_+\Vert_{\text{Eucl}} \leq \sqrt{I_M/2}$ — agreeing with Track 1 exactly (both bound the same Euclidean displacement). The prior-dominant ($\sigma\to\infty$) and likelihood-dominant ($\sigma\to 0$) limits both *vanish* the bound: the architecture either learns nothing from data or has no data-noise to be displaced through.

### What the universal constant buys ^sec-track2-buys

The contrast with Track 1 is sharp. Track 1 carries the post-update concentration constant $C_{T_2}$, which is family-specific and requires verification per architecture (LSI on the post-update law via Bakry-Émery / Otto-Villani; or dimension-free sub-Gaussian Lipschitz concentration via Gozlan; bare bounded support gives only the weaker $T_1$ fallback). Track 2 has *no* family-specific parameters: once the (PI) commitment is in place, the bound is $\sqrt{2}\sqrt{I_M}$ locally ([[#^thm-track2-uncond]], under (H4$'$) + (R) + (K)) or $(\pi/\sqrt 2)\sqrt{I_M}$ globally ([[#^thm-track2-glob]], under (PI) only), full stop. This is the role the (PI) commitment plays: it converts a family-dependent constant into a coordinate-invariant theorem-grade bound with universal constants — *one* universal constant locally tight at $\sqrt{2}$, *another* universal constant globally valid at $\pi/\sqrt 2$, with strictly nested hypotheses and the $\pi/2 \approx 1.57$ overhead of the global form being exactly the worst-case arc-chord ratio at the unit $L^2$-sphere's antipode.

A useful comparison: the Pinsker inequality $\Vert P - Q\Vert_{TV} \leq \sqrt{\mathrm{KL}/2}$ (Csiszár-Körner) gives a universal $\sqrt{1/2}$ constant on total-variation displacement, but $\Vert\cdot\Vert_{TV}$ is a coarse metric that loses the geometric fine-structure of $\mathcal{M}$ (and Pinsker is not generally tight — equality holds only in degenerate cases). The Fisher-Rao bounds [[#^eq-track2-uncond]] and [[#^eq-track2-glob]] are the *fine-structure analogs* of Pinsker — coordinate-free on the statistical manifold, second-order sharp at small $I_M$, with universal constants. The connection runs through Amari-Nagaoka's exponential-family Legendre-Fenchel geometry, which positions Fisher-Rao as the metric layer of the same Bregman geometry that grounds Pinsker. [[#^thm-hellinger]]'s Hellinger statement is the chord companion on the same unit $L^2$-sphere — universal constant $1/\sqrt 2$ on a chord, dual to [[#^thm-track2-glob]]'s $\pi/\sqrt 2$ on the arc, related by the global chord-arc bound ([[#^lem-chord-arc]]).

### Cite-and-distinguish ^sec-track2-cite

\citet{kurtek-bharath-2015} develop a Fisher-Rao framework for *Bayesian sensitivity analysis* — using the Fisher-Rao geodesic to assess local and global robustness of Bayesian procedures to perturbations of the likelihood and prior under an $\varepsilon$-contamination class. This is the closest existing Fisher-Rao deployment to ours. The targets differ: their target is sensitivity *to perturbations in an $\varepsilon$-contamination class*; ours is a *universal-constant bound on goal-conditional displacement* under (PI) + (R) + (K). Same geometric machinery; different theorem statement. \citet{mcculloch-1989} and \citet{ruggeri-wasserman-1993} develop earlier Fisher-information-flavored sensitivity analyses; both are foundational but neither produces a universal-constant bound for a coupled-architecture displacement.
